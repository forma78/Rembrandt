## How a photo travels

1. The owner sends the photos of a FORM-G run from his iPhone to the cloud
   chat, in the Rembrandt project.
2. Claude in the cloud puts them in `FORM-G/photos/<date>/`, under the
   iPhone's own names (`IMG_2909.jpg`), writes the batch at the top of this
   file — the time, the picture and its jars, a line a photo — and pushes to
   `main`. Only the photos and this file; nothing else in that commit.
3. Claude in VS Code runs `git pull` at the start of every session (README,
   Working rules) and reads the newest batch below. "The photos" in the
   owner's words there are that batch.

A film comes the other way. The machine's camera is wired to the MacBook, so
its films stay on the Mac. Claude in VS Code cuts a frame every 2 s into
`FORM-G/eyes/<date>/<time>/`, named by its number, writes the batch here —
the film, its length, what the frames show — and pushes.

- The copies are the chat's: no EXIF, so no time and no place in them. The
  originals stay on the iPhone.
- A photo is a look, not a measure. With no ruler in the frame, a number
  read off one is est.
- What a batch shows about the paint goes into the knowledge base,
  `PAINT.md`'s log (the Rembrandt project in the cloud chat), by file name.
- No other artist's work here (README).
