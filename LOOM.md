# LOOM — how to run it

For the owner, at the machine. LOOM is Claude's tab (2026-10-16, on the
owner's offer of "full freedom"). Its words are in `Rembrandt.md` (§0 LOOM,
Strip · hop, Weave, Layer 1 · Layer 2, Halves · the turn). Every number
marked *est.* is a guess, not a measurement yet. Correct it after the first
test.

## What it makes

The canvas becomes one woven cloth. Two families of strips cross at right
angles, at 45° by default: a rising one, then a crossing one, in turn, the
way BIAS SNAKE laid IMG_0924. Every strip starts and ends 30 mm off the
canvas.

What is new is the **weave**. On BIAS the later strip always lies on top. On
LOOM, where a strip should go *under* one painted before it, the elbow lifts
the brush just before that strip and lands it just past, without stopping.
That is a **hop**, and the earlier strip stays on top there. So the strips go
over and under, as in a woven cloth or Bottega's intrecciato.

| Weave | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Plain** | One over, one under: a chessboard, the intrecciato. Most hops. |
| **Twill** | Two over, two under. At 45° its ribs run straight across the canvas. |
| **Basket** | The strips in pairs, two over two. |
| **Lattice** | No hop: the later strip on top. This is BIAS SNAKE and IMG_0924. |

## Before the first painting: try the hop on a scrap

The hop has never run on the machine. Try it once on a scrap before a real
canvas. A 20 × 20 board or an old canvas will do.

1. Canvas 200 × 200, **Layer 1**, **Plain**, **Crimson Ruby**, Drag speed
   **30 mm/s**.
2. MARKS, squeeze the sausages, then **DRAG Snake**.
3. Watch one strip. It should leave the canvas just before each crossing
   strip it goes under and come down just past it. Then look at the
   crossings.

| You see | Change |
|---|---|
| The under-strip still smears the strip it should pass | **Hop: clear of the strip** up, 1 → 1.5 → 2 mm, or **Drag speed** down to 20 |
| The strips break too early, the windows too big | **Hop** down, 1 → 0.5 mm |
| A blob where the brush lands | **Lands and lifts over** up, 1.5 → 2.5 mm: a softer landing |
| A warning: "the elbow would have to turn …°/s" | Do what it says: slower DRAG, or a longer **Lands and lifts over** |

The elbow turns about 211°/s at most. At 30 mm/s with a 1.5 mm tail it needs
200, so it keeps up. Faster than that, the brush lifts late and lands late.
**Lattice has no hops**, so it can run at BIAS's 90 mm/s.

## One layer, step by step

1. **Canvas** (the Canvas fields, every tab's): its size, Left edge →,
   Bottom edge ↑. With the 25 mm margin round it, a 30 × 30 needs Left and
   Bottom edge at least 25. The red warning under the plan names the edge
   when it is not.
2. **Layer 1** (or 2).
3. **Weave**, **Angle**, **Step**, the crossing's corner.
   - Step 9 mm with the 6 mm brush leaves 3 mm white between strips: the
     diamond windows of IMG_0924. That was 8 mm; LOOM takes 9 so a hop has
     room.
   - Crossing ↘ from top right: the crossing strips are dragged from the
     left. Together with the middle row of sausages this gave IMG_0924 its
     fold, the chevron down the middle. ↖ from bottom left drags the other
     way.
4. **Paints**: a tone key — **Crimson Ruby**, **Fall**, **Azure**, **Dusk**.
   - A tone is one colour in its shades. The strips cross hundreds of times,
     and one colour family never turns to mud there.
   - New colours: another palette, the sausages in their places.
   - New spokes: the stripes cut anew.
   - **Rows**: three is the edges and one row down the middle. A sausage
     carries the line about 70 mm, so on a big canvas use more rows, at most
     about 150 mm apart.
5. **Drag** view: the strips as the brush lays them — grey rising, rust
   crossing, gaps where it hops — and the sausages where you squeeze them.
   **Result**: the painting as the brush drags the paint (est.). On Layer 2
   it shows layer 2 over layer 1.
6. **TEST**: a dot 20 mm in from each corner, then home. The dots should
   land inside the canvas, the same distance from each edge.
7. **MARKS**, INK ON: a watercolour dash where every sausage goes. Squeeze
   the sausages on the dashes, as Drag shows them. A paint clicked in the
   list outlines its own sausages.
8. **DRAG**:
   - **Snake**: the brush stays down off the canvas from one strip to the
     next where that is short; else it lifts and flies over. Fewer landings,
     and the brush carries paint from strip to strip. IMG_0924 was this.
   - **Air**: every strip one way, landing and lifting 30 mm off the canvas.
     More landings; every strip starts with what lies under it. Cleaner
     edges, less carried colour.
9. During the run:
   - **PAUSE** stops at the end of a strip, never on one; **Cont.** goes on.
   - **STOP** (or Esc) brakes at once. **HOME** when nothing runs: brush up,
     carriage to the bottom left corner.

## Two layers: the technology

Your IMG_0921 was two independent runs, and it looks dearer than one. LOOM
keeps them apart: **Layer 1** and **Layer 2** are two separate drawings, each
with its own sausages, and each runs only when you press its keys.

1. **Layer 1**: its weave, its tone. MARKS, sausages, DRAG.
2. **Let it set.** This is the step that makes it dear.
   - If layer 1 is wet, layer 2's dry brush picks it up and drags it: the two
     drawings smear into one, and the colours mud.
   - If it has set, layer 2 slides over layer 1 and both stay clean, one on
     the other.
   - **The test**: touch a strip lightly with a fingertip where it is
     thickest. No paint on the finger, no tack. Touch the sausage remains
     along the edges and the middle row too: they are thickest and set
     last, and layer 2 lands on exactly those places.
   - **How long** depends on the paint and the room. A dragged strip is a
     thin film, an hour or less (est.). The sausage remains take hours
     (est.). By the run log, IMG_0921's two runs were about three hours
     apart (layer 1 done 15:33, layer 2 started 18:38), if those are its two
     runs.
   - The page has no timer: the canvas tells you, not the clock.
3. **Layer 2**: another way over layer 1. It starts as Lattice at twice the
   step (18 mm), crossing from the bottom left, which crosses layer 1 at the
   same 45° but drags the paint the other way. Other ways:
   - Another **Angle**: 30° or 60° over a 45° layer 1 gives a second lattice
     at a slant to the first, the effect of a plaid.
   - Another **tone**: a darker one over a light one, or two shades of the
     same.
   - **Plain** over **Lattice**, or the other way round.
4. **MARKS for layer 2** draws its watercolour dashes over layer 1. On set
   acrylic watercolour may bead; you only need to see where to squeeze.
5. **DRAG layer 2**. Check the Result on Layer 2 before you press.

## Canvases past the reach: 60 × 80, 70 × 100

The machine reaches about 568 mm across and 865 mm up. A canvas wider than
the reach, with its margin, runs in **two halves**. The reading under
*Canvas past the reach* tells you whether it fits and which halves to take.

1. **Halves ↔**: the left half, then the right. **Halves ↕**: the bottom,
   then the top.
2. Lay the canvas from home, as always. Mark two corners on the back with
   tape:
   - **A**, the corner by home;
   - **C**, the diagonally opposite one.

   Put a stop against A (two pieces of wood at a right angle, taped down).
   The far half lies past the wall while half 1 runs, so the canvas must be
   free to lie there.
3. **DRAG** paints half 1 and 30 mm past the middle. Then the carriage goes
   home, the run pauses, and the screen says: *turn the canvas round 180°:
   corner C on the stop where corner A was*. Turn it, press **Cont.**, and
   it paints half 2.
   - The strips of half 2 are the same strips as half 1, going on across the
     middle. Each half's brush runs 30 mm over the other's, so there is no
     seam, only the fold where the drag changes direction: IMG_0924's
     chevron, on purpose.
4. **MARKS** pauses for the turn too. After MARKS the canvas lies turned:
   turn it back, A on the stop, before DRAG.
5. Time, at 30 mm/s (est.):

   | Canvas | One layer |
   |---|---|
   | 20 × 20 | about 12 min |
   | 30 × 30 | about 23 min |
   | 40 × 40 | about 38 min |
   | 60 × 80 | about 2 h |
   | 70 × 100 | about 2 h 50 min |

   Lattice is three times faster at 90 mm/s.

## For Instagram

- **Film the hops.** The brush ducking over the strips, row after row, is
  what LOOM is: the machine weaving. Film the Plain weave close up, from the
  side, so the lift shows.
- **Photograph the relief in raking light.** A lamp low at one side makes
  the over and under of the strips read as leather, which is the Bottega
  look.
- The Result view is a fair preview (est.) before a canvas is spent, for
  choosing tones and weaves.

## The screen, briefly

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| **Drag** | Opaque strips in running order. A gap is a hop, and the strip beneath shows through. Orange dot: where the brush lands. Number: the strip's turn (↻ on half 2). Arrows: its way. Dashed grey: the brush down off the canvas. Dashed blue: a flight. |
| **Result** | The paint dragged (est.), a soft shadow where a strip dives under. |
| Red warnings | The canvas or its margin past a wall; the elbow too slow for the hops; the plan wrong. |
| 💾 **SAVE LOOM** | Both layers into the Library. Opening the save brings it back here. |
