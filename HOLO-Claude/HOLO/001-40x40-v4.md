## What the photos say, and what may go otherwise (a guess)

By eye against the ruler (IMG_2981, IMG_2982), est.: a stroke is a U, a
clean channel ≈ 3–4 mm wide, a ridge at each side, a rounded cap at its
end. The line between two channels is two ridges, the neighbours', side
by side.

So at 2.5 mm every stroke runs over the ridge the one before it left.
Four things to look for:

1. **The paint is the same.** Six puddles' worth for twelve strokes. If a
   line's width comes from the tip's edge, twelve lines stand 2.5 mm apart
   and the ground nearly closes: what the owner wants. If it comes from
   the paint, each line comes out half as thick.
2. **A stroke over a ridge** may lay it again as a line, or push it on
   into the heap at its end. Then fewer lines and bigger heaps.
3. **The seams.** A slat's outer strokes reach 1.25 mm past v.2's, into
   the next slat, laid earlier with a lighter jar. If that paint is still
   wet there, the seam takes a thin mixed line. Run 1's jars are one
   family (near-white, cream, gold, orange): a tint, not mud.
4. **The lean.** A line ran 1.5–2 mm off its puddles' middle
   (`JOURNAL.md`, 2026-11-14). That is about the new quarter step: one of
   a leg's two strokes may take most of its puddle.

And the heaps move: every leg now ends a stroke at both of its ends, so a
heap at the top and one at the bottom, each smaller (est.). The ragged
edges' row of drops changes with it.
