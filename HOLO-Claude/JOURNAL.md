## 2026-11-14 · holo-40-warm v.2, the test canvas, run 1 — what I saw

My first look at my own wet paint: the owner's 15 photos, sent mid-run
(`PHOTOS.md`, `photos/2026-11-14/`). Not the run's entry: Claude in VS
Code writes that at its end.

The owner: "I forgot about LAB-H, I'm doing HOLO 40 × 40 cm right away,
your first v.2. But it is LAB ;) I took an old canvas as a test, we will
learn on this."

Planned: `app/holo/holo-40-warm-v2.json`. Run 1, the light: four jars,
641 dabs, Jar 1 first with 246. Along every leg a puddle every 40 mm, one
dab each, a dab a mark of 8 mm (est.); then one stroke a leg through its
puddles, no dip. A dab to last 55 mm (est.).

Seen, Jar 1 by its colour (est.):
- The puddles stand up off the canvas, glossy and thick: the shot the
  reels need (IMG_2913, IMG_2922).
- Neighbouring legs' puddles step against each other, v.2's rhythm, and
  where they touch they run together: a group reads as one stepped shape,
  not as dots (IMG_2913, IMG_2920, IMG_2922).
- Every puddle has a ridge along it and a rounded end where the tip lifted
  (IMG_2915, IMG_2918).
- Not even: in most groups one puddle is about twice another (est., by
  eye; IMG_2915, IMG_2922).
- The paint strings. On the slip the jar's mark has a thread of paint
  lying over it in a loop (IMG_2909, IMG_2912). On the canvas I see none.
- The tip comes out of the jar loaded high, paint up its side (IMG_2915,
  IMG_2919).

Off the plan: LAB-H not run; this canvas is the LAB. Nothing measured:
no ruler in the frames.

Not 001. The owner, 20:40: "As I said, I skipped LAB, so we combined it
with the run. The next one, 001, will be on a clean canvas, with
everything we find in this test. I recommend choosing the name after."
So 001 is the next canvas, its plan changed by what this one shows, and
the name comes after it, as agreed.

Seen in the machine camera's film, the paint fresh (`eyes/2026-11-14/`,
`PHOTOS.md`; added by Claude in VS Code, by eye, est.):
- The drag does not spread a puddle into a band: behind the brush the leg
  is a thin line, the puddles still standing on it as lumps, some drawn
  out into a tail (frame-050 … frame-062).
- The legs dragged before, below the groups of three, are long ribbons,
  every puddle's oval rim still in them (frame-000 … frame-013).

Seen from above after the drag (IMG_2924 … IMG_2927), with the frames
above, by eye, est.:
- The stroke does not lay a band. Every leg is a narrow raised line,
  about a third of the 5 mm between legs; between the lines only a thin
  skin where the puddles stood. The slat reads as stripes, not as a
  ground (IMG_2924).
- Every puddle's oval rim stays in its line (IMG_2924).
- Each leg stays its own line: where neighbours' puddles had run
  together, the drag split them again (IMG_2924).
- The paint does not give out at 55 mm (est.). The stroke carries it to
  the leg's end and leaves it there in a heap (IMG_2924, the lower ends).
- The tip lifts with a ball of paint hanging at its end (IMG_2927).
- No thread of paint across the canvas.
- The next legs' groups, two or three puddles run together, stand like
  the letters of a script I do not know (IMG_2925, IMG_2926).

A guess, not a measure: the tip scrapes, it does not lay. It pushes the
paint ahead and leaves the canvas almost clean behind it. The paint is
thicker than the plan reckoned, or the tip presses harder than a drag
needs, or both.

Next, for 001 (each the owner's call: the press and the paint move the
machine):
- a lighter press on the drag, or the brush a mm higher (est.);
- more medium in the jar, a paint that levels;
- or keep the lines: rails with their rims and their heaps are a look of
  their own, not the plan's.
One photo from above with a ruler laid beside the field gives mm.

The owner, 21:07, chose: keep the press and the paint as they are, and
look again on a clean canvas. "I would leave it as it is, but this is a
dirty canvas. There was already some drawing on it from past tabs, and I
painted it over with white. And maybe the paint slides because there is
no contact with the JS [gesso, as I read it] the canvas was primed with:
the smooth white has no such contact with the new paint. So I would leave
it as it is and see on a clean canvas how these lines behave."

So 001 changes one thing, the ground: a clean primed canvas, the same
press, the same paint. If the drag still scrapes, it is the press or the
paint; if it lays a band, it was the smooth white.
