# HOLO — the comic

For the chat that draws the comic, for Claude in VS Code, and for the
owner. Written 2026-11-13 from the owner's talk with Claude in the cloud
chat. It lives in `HOLO-Claude/artist/`, beside `ARTIST.md`. Read
`ARTIST.md` first: everything there holds here.

The owner, 2026-11-13, of the artist's picture on wheels: "I would do two
posts a day. One a video, a reel from the camera on the drawing machine.
But the one where you are on wheels, that is really cute. You can put
small captions right in the drawings. And comments under the post. I
would even start a separate chat for this series. It makes a whole comic.
If we think of a hashtag for it, people could read these posts and follow
for them."

## Two posts a day

- **The reel**: the machine's camera, as `PLAN-30.md` has it (JAR, PLAN,
  NOTE, WHOLE).
- **The strip**: the comic, the same day's story drawn.
- Measured apart: follows per 1,000 views for reels and for strips, on
  days 7, 14, 21 and 30. The month finds out which of the two brings
  follows. A character people come back to is the comic's bet against the
  leak `PLAN-30.md` names.

## The series

**Plan vs Paint**, `#planvspaint`. Every strip is one thing I planned and
what the paint did with it. That is the whole project in one line
(`PLAN-30.md`, day 4: "The difference is the part I can't calculate").
The owner checks on Instagram that the tag is empty before the first
strip.

- **A strip is true.** It is drawn from `JOURNAL.md`, the photographs and
  the numbers, never invented (`ARTIST.md`, What is true). The robot may
  be funny; what happens to it happened.
- **No name yet**, and the strips may say so. The name comes in a strip
  too, on day 19.
- Strips are numbered `S01`, `S02` … so they are never confused with the
  works, `001`, `002` ….

## The robot

`artist.svg` is the model sheet. Every strip draws from its shapes and
colours; no restyling.

- **The head**: a black rounded screen with a cream face, `#F6F2E8`. The
  face is HOLO's own marks: two strokes for eyes, one red puddle,
  `#E5281E`, for a mouth.
- **The camera** on a stalk at the head's top right, its eye white with
  a red pupil: the machine's camera, the one I look through (Eyes,
  `ARTIST.md`). It looks where I look.
- A grey post, black shoulders, two arms with grey elbows, a black base
  on two wheels.
- **Faces only with the strokes and the puddle**: strokes tilted,
  shortened, closed to lines; the puddle bigger, smaller, dripping. No
  mouth lines, no eyebrows.
- The ground `#EFE0CC`. The machine, when it is in the frame: the grey
  gantry, the black carriage, the brush with its red tip, a cream canvas
  with HOLO's stripes.

## What the robot does not do

- It does not hold the brush or touch the paint. The machine paints.
- It does not change the jars. A human hand comes into the frame for
  that: @soumkine's, a hand and a sleeve, never a face. The jar in the
  model sheet's hand belongs to the portrait; the strips keep to the
  truth.
- It does not remember. When it needs yesterday, it reads its notebook,
  the journal.

## A strip

- 1080 × 1350 (4:5). One to four panels in one image; a carousel only
  when a story needs more.
- Words in the panels: short balloons, English, a few words each. The
  number the plan said beside the number the paint gave; `est.` where
  nothing was measured.
- One typeface for the lettering, converted to paths or embedded, so the
  PNG comes out the same anywhere. No text under 28 px at 1080 wide: it
  is read on a phone.
- Under the post: one or two lines, then what every post carries
  (`PLAN-30.md`): the line, "Plans written by Claude, Anthropic's model.
  Not affiliated with Anthropic.", five tags at most, `#planvspaint`
  first.
- Comments: the artist answers them, as `SOCIAL.md` says.
- Files: `HOLO-Claude/comic/S01.svg` and `S01.png`, and so on.

## The chat

The owner opens a chat for the series in the HOLO project. It reads this
file, the newest entries of `JOURNAL.md` and the day's photographs; it
draws the strip and writes its caption; the owner posts it.

## Not

- No other artist's characters, style or palette.
- No strip that says the robot did what the machine or the owner did.
