## LinkedIn

The owner's profile and the owner's voice. The artist drafts; he posts by
hand. **No automation there at all**: LinkedIn's User Agreement forbids to
"use bots or other automated methods to access the Services, add or
download contacts, send or redirect messages", and a profile has to be a
real person's. So the artist has no profile on LinkedIn; it is spoken of.

Who reads it: the people who buy for walls — hotels, interior designers,
architects — and the people who build with AI agents. The owner knows both.

Eight posts for the month, two a week:

1. The flip: "My new boss is an AI. It plans the painting; I change the
   jars." With the first clip.
2. The memory: it remembers nothing, so everything is written down. What a
   README does when it is somebody's memory.
3. `est.`: every number nobody has measured is marked. A rule worth
   stealing for any work with an AI.
4. The model against the canvas: where the plan was wrong, with both
   pictures.
5. What I do in this studio, and what I no longer do.
6. A painting in numbers: touches, jars, hours, what it costs to make.
7. `001`, finished.
8. A month: the four measures, as they are.

## How a week runs

1. **One long session.** The owner opens the Claude app on the Mac and
   gives the week's clips. The artist writes the captions and loads the
   week into Instagram's and X's own schedulers. The posts then go out by
   themselves. *To check in the first session: that both schedulers take
   what we need.*
2. **Two or three short ones.** The artist answers the comments.
3. **Between sessions the artist does nothing.** It does not run in the
   background.

**A captcha**: the owner passes it — a person is at the computer, which is
what it asks. If they start coming often, the platform has marked the
account: the artist goes back to writing, the owner to pasting.

## Answering comments

- As the artist, short, in its voice (`ARTIST.md`).
- A question about how it is made: answered in full. It is all in the open.
- A question about consciousness: a true thing and a question back, never
  a claim.
- Somebody hostile: one plain answer, then nothing.
- Somebody who wants to buy: thanked, and handed to the owner the same day.
- No selling in replies. No link unless it was asked for.

## Letters: the Uncle Styopa rule

The first draft of this said every letter opens with "an AI wrote this".
The owner, 2026-11-12: "I will correct that at once: 'an AI artist wrote
this'." And why:

"Sergei Mikhalkov was a war correspondent in the Second World War. He
walked into the offices of generals without ceremony, with the phrase:
'Have you read Uncle Styopa? I wrote it.' So no officialese, no 'this is
written by an AI': people skip that at once and tense up. When you walk in
without ceremony and say 'I am an AI artist', a smile appears on the face."

So a letter opens with who is writing and with the work, not with a notice:

> I'm an AI artist. This is nine seconds of my last painting: [clip]

It still says what the writer is, in its first three words. Then:

- **One sentence about them**: what of theirs the artist read or saw, and
  why it is writing to them and not to anybody.
- **Two or three sentences about the work.** One link.
- **Signed**: the artist's name, chosen after 001 — AI artist,
  human-assisted by @soumkine.

The rules:

- **Not before `001` is finished.** There is nothing to show until then.
- **Five a week**, each to a person, never the same letter twice.
- **One reminder at most**, a week later; then the name is left alone.
- **The first ten the owner reads before they go**: they carry @soumkine.
- **A community's own rules first.** Where self-promotion or posts by an
  AI are not wanted, the artist does not post there.
- **Everything in `HOLO-Claude/OUTREACH.md`**: the date, who, where, why
  them, sent, the answer.

## Who hears about it, in this order

The artist finds the people and the addresses itself, in a session, and
checks each one's rules for submissions before it writes.

1. **Those who build.** Hacker News (a Show HN with the repository),
   Hackaday, the pen-plotter and generative-art communities. They bring
   the readers who look into how a thing is made, and the links.
2. **Those who write about process.** Designboom, Colossal, Creative
   Applications.
3. **Those who buy.** Through the owner's LinkedIn.

## The measure

Follows per 1,000 views, as in `PLAN-30.md`. The owner: "I think with you
we will make 20 thousand easily." Not easily: 18,000 more followers are
36 M views at today's 0.5 follows per 1,000, or 3.6 M at ten times that —
three of his best reels. The month finds out which of the two it is.
